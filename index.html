01 · The problem

Fraud is so rare that accuracy lies

Only 0.58% of transactions are fraudulent. A model that predicts legitimate, every single time scores 99.6% accuracy and catches nothing. So the real questions are business questions: how much fraud can you catch, how many good customers do you disturb to catch it, and how fast can you act when you do.

02 · Prevent

Half the charges between $800 and $1,200 are fraud

Fraud does not spread evenly across purchase sizes. It piles into one narrow window. Hover the bars to see each band; the red window speaks for itself.

50.91%
fraud rate inside $800 to $1,200. That is 98x the 0.52% baseline, across 6,783 transactions.
The move: for online charges inside this window, trigger an automatic 10 second verification before the charge goes through. The fraud dies at the checkout, before any money moves. No analyst, no model, no queue.
Said honestly: the window is this sharp because the data is simulated. Real fraud concentrates by amount too, just less cleanly. What transfers is the method: find your portfolio's window, verify inside it, recalibrate on live data.

04 · Respond

92.5% of the money is stolen after the first hit

Fraud arrives as a burst. A compromised card takes a median of 10 fraudulent charges, the second one landing about 1.1 hours after the first. The bank is not losing money because fraud is hard to detect. It is losing money in the hours after the first detection, while the alert waits in a queue.

The data sets the deadline: confirm the first alert within about an hour, because the median second hit lands 1.1 hours later. The model's job is to surface that first hit fast: 86% of them already rank inside its top 1% of scores.

05 · Protect

99.1% of customers never feel any of this

Here is one average day: about 2,879 transactions. The system quietly scores all of them and asks a human to look at 29. Everyone else's purchase goes through untouched, and the few who are flagged get a 10 second verification, never an automatically declined card.

This matters commercially, not just politely: a wrongly declined customer is embarrassed at the register, the purchase revenue is lost, and many quietly switch to the other card in their wallet for good. Precision is not a modelling nicety. It is how you keep the customers you were protecting.

Do not buy a bigger model. Buy a faster confirmation.

Read this before quoting the numbers

The data is simulated (Sparkov, 1.85M transactions, 2019 to 2020), which makes patterns cleaner than live card fraud. Dollar benefits are illustrative upper bounds: they treat every flagged fraud dollar as saved and assume instant confirmation.

The prevent, detect and respond layers act on overlapping dollars. They compose into one defense; they do not add up.

Every figure on this page comes from executed code on the full 555,719 transaction holdout: model metrics from a clean top to bottom run, the review sweep from the notebook's printed table, and band rates from a deterministic recount of all 1,852,394 transactions.
