01 · The problem

A model that does nothing scores 99.6% accuracy

Only 0.58% of transactions are fraudulent. So a model that just predicts legitimate, every single time gets a 99.6% accuracy score while catching zero fraud. That is why we never grade on accuracy here. The real questions are business questions: how much fraud can you catch, how many good customers do you disturb to catch it, and how fast can you act when you do.

02 · Prevent

Half the charges between $800 and $1,200 are fraud

Fraud does not spread evenly across purchase sizes. It piles into one narrow window. Hover the bars to see each band; the red window speaks for itself.

50.91%
fraud rate inside $800 to $1,200. That is 98x the 0.52% baseline, across 6,783 transactions.
The move: for online charges inside this window, trigger an automatic 10 second verification before the charge goes through. The fraud dies at the checkout, before any money moves. No analyst, no model, no queue.
Said honestly: the window is this sharp because the data is simulated. Real fraud concentrates by amount too, just less cleanly. What transfers is the method: find your portfolio's window, verify inside it, recalibrate on live data.

04 · Respond · the confirmation speed problem

92.5% of the money is stolen after the first hit

Fraud arrives as a burst. A compromised card takes a median of 10 fraudulent charges, the second one landing about 1.1 hours after the first. The bank is not losing money because fraud is hard to detect. It is losing money in the hours after the first detection, while the alert waits in a queue. This is the confirmation speed problem the title promised. Drag the confirmation delay: the slower the bank confirms, the more of the burst gets through.

05 · Protect

99.1% of customers never feel any of this

This matters commercially, not just politely: a wrongly declined customer is embarrassed at the register, the purchase revenue is lost, and many quietly switch to the other card in their wallet for good. Precision is not a modelling nicety. It is how you keep the customers you were protecting.

06 · What we would ship

The policy, felt from the checkout

The four moves become one decision engine, and we would ship it as two features: a quick check at the checkout, and an alert on the cardholder's phone. Try both. The logic is distilled from the published analysis for illustration; production thresholds would be recalibrated on live data.

FEATURE 2 · ON THE CARDHOLDER'S PHONE
The fastest analyst is the cardholder

There is a third way to confirm, quicker than any queue: ask the one person who knows. The alert names the merchant and shows its measured fraud rate from our watchlist, so the customer decides with context. One tap on No and the card is frozen in under a minute, long before the median second hit at 1.1 hours. The analyst queue stays as the fallback for alerts nobody answers.
